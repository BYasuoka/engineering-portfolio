Thermal Ribbon Respooling System
Automation Engineer Intern | Kodak Alaris | Summer 2025

PROJECT OVERVIEW

During my internship with Kodak Alaris, I designed and implemented a custom automated thermal ribbon respooling system that transformed a manually intensive prototype process into a repeatable production-ready machine.

The system was developed to identify and isolate defects in large rolls of thermal transfer ribbon. Previously, operators had limited options when defects were discovered in a roll, often requiring significant manual effort to locate and recover usable material. The new system allows operators to accurately rewind and reposition ribbon to a desired location, enabling efficient defect isolation and reducing material waste.

The project progressed from concept development through final deployment and involved mechanical design, controls programming, motion control, electrical integration, pneumatics, and operator interface development.

PROBLEM STATEMENT

Kodak Alaris produces thermal transfer ribbon in rolls approximately 1,000 feet long.

When a defect was discovered within a roll, operators needed a reliable way to:
- Locate the defect position
- Rewind the material to a precise location
- Separate defective material from usable material
- Minimize operator time and material waste

Existing solutions were either overly large, costly, or not optimized for this specific workflow.

The goal was to create a compact automated machine capable of accurately tracking ribbon position while providing a simple operator experience.

MAJOR TECHNICAL CHALLENGES

Encoder Signal Compatibility

Initial Condition
- Encoder output: NPN
- PLC input expectation: PNP

Solution
- Implemented a relay interface to convert encoder signals into a PLC-compatible format

Long-Distance Position Accuracy

Challenge
- Small counting errors accumulate over long distances

Result
- Approximately ±5 mm accuracy over 10 meters of travel

Motion Command Interface

Solution
- Implemented a 4-bit command architecture between PLC and motor controller
- Allowed 16 possible command combinations
- 15 defined machine actions
